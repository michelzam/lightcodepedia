```
### 🌏 Why this course — motivation

The world runs on data, quietly behind the scenes. Hospitals,
factories, schools, local shops, food 🍕 — survival!

A few big apps are used by millions. **Most software is
custom-built** — and evolving. Every year the world produces about
**147 zettabytes** of data, doubling every four years.

The data industry is worth trillions and shapes modern economies.
Billions of people touch databases daily — even if they never see
one. The purpose of this course: keep information **safe, usable,
and ready when needed**.

5 billion consumers, 50 million builders. App builders,
developers-in-training — **this is where you come in** 🫵
```

```
### 🎯 What you will be able to do

Upon completion of the course, you will be able to:

1. **Articulate** important concepts in database management, across
   database types.
2. **Query** databases and generate reports with growing proficiency.
3. **Model** data for RDBMS and other database types.
4. **Create** a database from entities, a data model, or business
   requirements collected from stakeholders.
5. **Evaluate** database systems for specific projects and document
   designs for stakeholders and end users.
6. **Address** multilingual and multicultural issues in database
   creation and maintenance.
7. **Identify** emerging trends and stay current with the field.
```

```
### 🧠 How we learn here

Every time you make a mistake, a synapse fires — that is Jo Boaler
quoting Carol Dweck, and it is the pedagogy of this class:
mistakes and struggles are the material, not the enemy.

Immediate feedback loops, learning by touching, questions asked out
loud, social interaction. And a trick from **classical drama**:
Aristotle's three unities — one place, one action, one time. Each
lesson is one small story, complete in itself.

That is why these pages have a guide who talks, tables you can
touch, and quizzes you can take twice.
```

```
### 🧭 Rules of the road

- **Canvas is home base** — announcements, materials, submissions,
  and this syllabus all live there.
- Email etiquette: subject line with the topic and your section,
  your full name in the body, what you tried, what you think the
  answer is.
- I answer within 24–48 hours Monday–Thursday, by Monday afternoon
  for weekend mail. No answer? Resend — it is the rule, not rudeness.
- Fairness: no extra-credit work unless the whole class gets the
  same chance (UWM Faculty Policy 1927).
- **AI**: welcome only where a task explicitly allows it, and always
  with citation. The work here is built so that doing beats
  delegating — an AI that never took the walk fails the checks.
- Your work is your own. Reused or borrowed work costs a letter
  grade, then another. You have been formally, warmly warned.
```
